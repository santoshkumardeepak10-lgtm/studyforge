Number Systems

The number system becomes more powerful in Class 9 because we move beyond rational numbers and study irrational numbers, real numbers, decimal expansions and rationalisation.

📌 Rational vs Irrational

  • Rational numbers can be written as p/q, where q ≠ 0.
  • Irrational numbers cannot be expressed as p/q.
  • √2, √3 and π are irrational.
  • A rational number has a terminating or recurring decimal expansion.
  • An irrational number has a non-terminating, non-recurring decimal expansion.

📌 Important Idea

  • Every rational and irrational number is a real number.
  • Between any two rational numbers, infinitely many rational numbers exist.
  • Between any two real numbers, infinitely many real numbers exist.
  • √n is irrational when n is not a perfect square.
🧠 Solved Example:

Is √50 rational or irrational?

√50 = √(25 × 2) = 5√2.

Since √2 is irrational, 5√2 is also irrational.

🧠 Rationalisation Example:

Consider 1/√5.

Multiply numerator and denominator by √5:

1/√5 × √5/√5 = √5/5

So the denominator no longer contains a radical.

🎯 Exam Tip: Do not simply say “√50 is irrational because it contains a root.” First simplify it to 5√2.

Polynomials

Polynomials combine variables, constants and powers. In Class 9, you learn how to identify their degree, find zeros and use identities.

📌 Degree & Zeros

  • The highest power of the variable is the degree.
  • A zero is a value of x for which p(x)=0.
  • A non-zero constant polynomial has degree 0.
  • A linear polynomial generally has one zero.

📌 Useful Identities

  • (a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
  • (a-b)² = a² - 2ab + b²
  • a²-b² = (a-b)(a+b)
  • (x+a)(x+b)=x²+(a+b)x+ab
🧠 Solved Example:

Find the zero of p(x)=2x-6.

2x-6=0

2x=6

x=3

🧠 Identity Example:

Calculate 103² quickly.

103²=(100+3)²

=10000+600+9

=10609

Coordinate Geometry

Coordinate Geometry connects algebra with geometry using ordered pairs.

📌 Cartesian Plane

  • Horizontal line = x-axis.
  • Vertical line = y-axis.
  • Intersection = origin O(0,0).
  • Plane has four quadrants.

📌 Quadrants

  • I → (+,+)
  • II → (-,+)
  • III → (-,-)
  • IV → (+,-)

📌 Special Points

  • x-axis → y=0
  • y-axis → x=0
  • Origin → (0,0)
  • (0,5) lies on y-axis.

📌 Coordinate Order

  • x-coordinate is written first.
  • y-coordinate is written second.
  • Same x → vertical line.
  • Same y → horizontal line.
🧠 Example:

Determine the quadrant of A(-5,3).

x is negative and y is positive.

Therefore A lies in the II quadrant.

🎯 Exam Tip: Remember: x first, y second.

Linear Equations in Two Variables

A linear equation in two variables has variables whose highest power is 1. Its solutions can be represented graphically.

📌 General Form

  • ax + by + c = 0
  • a and b cannot both be zero.
  • Generally, infinitely many solutions exist.
  • Every solution represents a point on the line.

📌 Finding Solutions

  • Choose x.
  • Substitute into the equation.
  • Find y.
  • Write the ordered pair.
🧠 Example:

Find two solutions of x+y=6.

x=0 → y=6 → (0,6)

x=2 → y=4 → (2,4)

Lines & Angles

This chapter is mainly about recognising angle relationships and applying them logically.

📌 Core Relationships

  • Linear pair → sum = 180°.
  • Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • Complementary angles → sum = 90°.
  • Supplementary angles → sum = 180°.

📌 Parallel Lines

  • Corresponding angles are equal.
  • Alternate interior angles are equal.
  • Co-interior angles are supplementary.
🧠 Example:

One angle of a linear pair is 65°.

Other angle = 180°−65°

=115°

Triangles

Triangles are studied through sides, angles and congruence. The key skill is identifying the correct congruence criterion.

📌 Congruence Rules

  • SSS → three corresponding sides equal.
  • SAS → two sides and included angle equal.
  • ASA → two angles and included side equal.
  • RHS → right angle, hypotenuse and one side equal.

📌 Important Properties

  • Angle sum of a triangle = 180°.
  • Equal sides have equal opposite angles.
  • Greater side has greater opposite angle.
  • Greater angle has greater opposite side.

Heron's Formula

Heron's formula allows us to find the area of a triangle when all three side lengths are known.

📌 Formula:

s = (a+b+c)/2

Area = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)]

🧠 Example:

For sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm:

s=(3+4+5)/2=6

Area=√[6×3×2×1]

=√36

=6 cm²

Surface Areas & Volumes

This chapter deals with surface area and volume of common three-dimensional objects.

📌 Cuboid

  • TSA = 2(lb+bh+hl)
  • Volume = lbh

📌 Cube

  • TSA = 6a²
  • Volume = a³

📌 Cylinder

  • CSA = 2πrh
  • TSA = 2πr(r+h)
  • Volume = πr²h

📌 Sphere

  • Surface Area = 4πr²
  • Volume = 4/3 πr³

Statistics

Statistics helps us organise, represent and interpret numerical data.

📌 Mean

  • Mean = Sum of observations / Number of observations.

📌 Median

  • Arrange observations in ascending order.
  • For odd number: middle observation.
  • For even number: average of two middle observations.

📌 Mode

  • Mode is the observation occurring most frequently.

Probability

Probability describes how likely an event is to occur.

📌 Basic Formula:

Probability of an event = Number of favourable outcomes / Total number of equally likely outcomes

📌 Range

  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
  • P(E)=0 means impossible event.
  • P(E)=1 means certain event.

📌 Example

  • A fair die has 6 outcomes.
  • Probability of getting 3 = 1/6.

Important Proofs & Theorems

This section contains important Class 9 geometry proofs in step-by-step exam-ready form. Learn the reason behind every step.

📐 1. Linear Pair of Angles

Statement: If two adjacent angles form a straight line, their sum is 180°.

Let ∠1 and ∠2 form a linear pair.

They together form a straight angle.

Therefore, ∠1 + ∠2 = 180° (Straight Angle)

Hence proved.

📐 2. Vertically Opposite Angles are Equal

Statement: When two lines intersect, vertically opposite angles are equal.

Let lines AB and CD intersect at O.

∠AOC + ∠AOD = 180° (Linear Pair)

∠AOD + ∠BOD = 180° (Linear Pair)

Therefore,

∠AOC + ∠AOD = ∠AOD + ∠BOD

Subtracting ∠AOD from both sides:

∠AOC = ∠BOD

Similarly, ∠AOD = ∠BOC.

Hence proved.

📏 3. Corresponding Angles

Statement: If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, corresponding angles are equal.

Let l ∥ m and a transversal t intersect them.

Consider a pair of corresponding angles ∠1 and ∠2.

Using the angle relationships formed by the transversal and the parallel lines, the corresponding angles have equal measure.

Therefore, ∠1 = ∠2.

This property is used extensively in Class 9 angle proofs.

📏 4. Alternate Interior Angles

Statement: When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, alternate interior angles are equal.

Let l ∥ m and t be a transversal.

Let ∠1 and ∠2 be alternate interior angles.

Because the two lines are parallel, the angle relationships created by the transversal give equal alternate interior angles.

Therefore, ∠1 = ∠2.

Remember: alternate interior angles lie between the parallel lines on opposite sides of the transversal.

🔺 5. Angle Sum Property of a Triangle

Statement: The sum of the three angles of a triangle is 180°.

In △ABC, draw a line through A parallel to BC.

Since the line through A is parallel to BC:

∠XAB = ∠ABC (Alternate Interior Angles)

∠CAY = ∠ACB (Alternate Interior Angles)

XAY is a straight line.

Therefore:

∠XAB + ∠BAC + ∠CAY = 180°

Substituting the equal angles:

∠ABC + ∠BAC + ∠ACB = 180°

Hence proved.

🔺 6. Exterior Angle Theorem

Statement: An exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of its two opposite interior angles.

In △ABC, extend BC to D.

From the angle sum property:

∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°

Since ∠ACD and ∠C form a linear pair:

∠ACD + ∠C = 180°

Comparing both equations:

∠ACD = ∠A + ∠B

Hence proved.

🔺 7. SSS Congruence Criterion

Statement: If three corresponding sides of two triangles are equal, the triangles are congruent.

Consider △ABC and △DEF.

Suppose:

AB = DE

BC = EF

CA = FD

All three corresponding sides are equal.

Therefore:

△ABC ≅ △DEF (SSS)

Hence their corresponding parts are equal.

🔺 8. SAS Congruence Criterion

Statement: If two sides and the included angle of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and included angle of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

Consider △ABC and △DEF.

AB = DE

∠B = ∠E

BC = EF

The equal angle is included between the two equal sides.

Therefore:

△ABC ≅ △DEF (SAS)

🔺 9. ASA Congruence Criterion

Statement: If two angles and the included side of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two angles and included side of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

Suppose in △ABC and △DEF:

∠A = ∠D

AB = DE

∠B = ∠E

The side lies between the two equal angles.

Therefore:

△ABC ≅ △DEF (ASA)

🔺 10. RHS Congruence Criterion

Statement: In two right triangles, if the hypotenuse and one corresponding side are equal, the triangles are congruent.

Consider two right triangles ABC and DEF.

∠B = ∠E = 90°

AC = DF (Hypotenuse)

AB = DE (One corresponding side)

Therefore:

△ABC ≅ △DEF (RHS)

🔺 11. Isosceles Triangle Theorem

Statement: If two sides of a triangle are equal, the angles opposite those sides are equal.

In △ABC, let:

AB = AC (Given)

Draw angle bisector AD of ∠A.

∠BAD = ∠DAC (Angle Bisector)

AD = AD (Common Side)

Therefore:

△ABD ≅ △ACD (SAS)

Therefore:

∠B = ∠C (CPCT)

Hence proved.

▱ 12. Opposite Sides of a Parallelogram

Statement: Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal.

Let ABCD be a parallelogram. Draw diagonal AC.

AB ∥ CD

Therefore:

∠BAC = ∠DCA (Alternate Interior Angles)

AD ∥ BC

Therefore:

∠BCA = ∠DAC (Alternate Interior Angles)

AC = AC (Common Side)

Therefore:

△ABC ≅ △CDA (ASA)

Hence:

AB = CD

and

BC = AD.

▱ 13. Opposite Angles of a Parallelogram

Statement: Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal.

Let ABCD be a parallelogram.

AB ∥ CD.

Therefore:

∠A + ∠B = 180°

Also AD ∥ BC, so:

∠B + ∠C = 180°

Therefore:

∠A + ∠B = ∠B + ∠C

Subtracting ∠B:

∠A = ∠C

Similarly:

∠B = ∠D

Hence proved.

▱ 14. Diagonals of a Parallelogram Bisect Each Other

Statement: The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

Let diagonals AC and BD of parallelogram ABCD intersect at O.

From the opposite-side property:

AB = CD

Since AB ∥ CD:

∠ABO = ∠CDO (Alternate Interior Angles)

∠BAO = ∠DCO (Alternate Interior Angles)

Therefore:

△AOB ≅ △COD (ASA)

Hence:

AO = OC

and

BO = OD.

Therefore the diagonals bisect each other.

⭕ 15. Perpendicular from Centre to a Chord Bisects the Chord

Statement: The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.

Let AB be a chord of a circle with centre O.

OM ⟂ AB.

OA = OB (Radii of same circle)

OM = OM (Common Side)

∠OMA = ∠OMB = 90°

Therefore:

△OMA ≅ △OMB (RHS)

Hence:

AM = MB (CPCT)

Thus OM bisects chord AB.

⭕ 16. Equal Chords are Equidistant from the Centre

Statement: Equal chords of a circle are equidistant from the centre.

Let AB and CD be equal chords of a circle with centre O.

Draw OM ⟂ AB and ON ⟂ CD.

From the perpendicular-chord theorem:

AM = MB

CN = ND

Since AB = CD:

AM = CN

Also:

OA = OC (Radii)

∠OMA = ∠ONC = 90°

Therefore:

△OMA ≅ △ONC (RHS)

Hence:

OM = ON.

Therefore the chords are equidistant from the centre.

⭕ 17. Equal Chords Subtend Equal Angles at Centre

Statement: Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre.

Let AB and CD be equal chords of a circle with centre O.

OA = OC (Radii)

OB = OD (Radii)

AB = CD (Given)

Therefore:

△AOB ≅ △COD (SSS)

Hence:

∠AOB = ∠COD (CPCT)

Hence proved.

🔷 18. Interior Angle Sum of a Polygon

Statement: The sum of the interior angles of an n-sided polygon is (n−2)×180°.

Take an n-sided polygon.

From one vertex, draw diagonals to all non-adjacent vertices.

The polygon is divided into:

(n−2) triangles

Each triangle has angle sum 180°.

Therefore:

Interior Angle Sum = (n−2)×180°

Hence proved.

🔺 19. Greater Side has Greater Opposite Angle

Statement: In a triangle, the side opposite the greater angle is greater.

In △ABC, suppose:

∠B > ∠C.

The side opposite ∠B is AC.

The side opposite ∠C is AB.

Therefore:

AC > AB.

Thus, the greater angle has the greater opposite side.

🔺 20. Greater Angle has Greater Opposite Side

Statement: In a triangle, the greater side has the greater opposite angle.

In △ABC, suppose:

AC > AB.

AC is opposite ∠B.

AB is opposite ∠C.

Therefore:

∠B > ∠C.

Hence proved.

📐 21. Two Lines Parallel to the Same Line are Parallel

Statement: If two lines are parallel to the same line, then they are parallel to each other.

Suppose:

l ∥ m

and

n ∥ m.

A transversal creates corresponding angles with the parallel lines.

Therefore the corresponding angles made by l and n are equal.

Hence:

l ∥ n.

Hence proved.

🎯 PROOF WRITING FORMULA:

Given → Construction → Angle/Side relation → Congruence → CPCT → Required result

For Class 9 geometry, always write the reason: Given, Common Side, Linear Pair, Alternate Interior Angles, SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS, CPCT.
